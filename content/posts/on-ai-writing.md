# On AI Writing

[There are no lossless transformations of natural language text](https://sophiebits.com/2026/06/25/there-are-no-lossless-transformations-of-natural-language-text) is a great  piece of writing on using AI to communicate, but I have a few points that I would add to it.

I try to follow the golden rule of "anything intended to be read by a human (with you as an author) should be written by a human". I should note, I've read other people use phrases similar to this but I think it is general enough that it doesn't require any citation. This rule feels like basic courtesy. If I want people to value my opinion they should read them in my voice. 

Sometimes, I try to use AI to improve or fact check my technical writing, and depending on what the output is, the level changes (personal blog post: No AI checks, Developer community post: AI fact checking and syntax checking, Official output e.g. docs/tutorials: extensive AI checks). But the key thing is that anything with my name should be written by me. The sentence **You must stand behind every idea and every sentence in your docs** in the piece I referenced at the start, is I think vital, and should be true of everything you put your name to. 

## AI Writing Smells

AI writing smells. After reading a bit, you start to get the distant hint of "this smells like ai". For me, that moment is the point at which I lose interest. Sure, a good AI model often writes more cleanly than I do. There's generally fewer typos, or slight errors in grammar, but instead it becomes monotonous. All of the embellishments of AI trying to make writing sound dramatic or interesting actually just make it sound like everything else on the internet which is trying to be dramatic or to get your attention. 

But its not just about losing interest, I also just find it harder to read. Somehow I just struggle to focus on AI written work like I would do human written work. 

This isn't to say AI writing cannot be good, its more that if an AI writes a page based off a one sentence prompt, then the intent of the piece either has one sentence of actual meaning, or is a statistically average prediction of the piece itself. 



## AI Bug Reports 

One place where I have sent AI writing to other people is when reporting bugs. This feels somewhat reasonable because AI is one of the first places I turn to to identify and confirm a bug. However, AI knowledge is uneven. It can be absolutely confident that there is a bug, because it didn't read the massive banner across the first page of the documentation on a topic. 

If you send an AI generated bug report by itself, I don't think it reflects well on you, because rather than take the time to read, understand and reproduce the error yourself, you are putting that time on someone else. My aim is to write a description of the error myself and then also share an Agents' description. This proves that A) I understand and believe what I am doing and B) I've taken the time to reproduce it with a tool that is unevenly super-intelligent. Whether I do this because I want people to like me, respect me, or just to be helpful, I can't say for sure.
